Exploring Japan

Japan has a huge range of experiences, from neon-lit city neighborhoods to quiet temples, mountain towns, beaches, and rural villages. Tokyo is known for areas like Shibuya, Asakusa, and Shinjuku, while Kyoto offers historic streets, shrines, gardens, and traditional architecture. Travelers who venture beyond the major cities can also find national parks, hot spring towns, coastal communities, and smaller destinations with a very different pace.

Food & Culture

Food is a major part of traveling in Japan. Depending on where you go, you might encounter sushi, ramen, udon, regional specialties, street food, or traditional multi-course meals. Japanese cuisine often emphasizes seasonal ingredients and careful presentation. Travelers should also be aware of a few customs, such as removing shoes in certain spaces, keeping voices low on public transportation, and paying attention to local dining etiquette.

Transportation

Japan has an extensive public transportation system that includes local trains, subways, buses, and the high-speed shinkansen. Major cities are especially easy to navigate without a car, and the rail network connects many destinations throughout the country. Visitors should expect orderly boarding, clearly marked stations, and quieter behavior on trains than they may be used to at home..
